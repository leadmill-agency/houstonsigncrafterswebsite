## What actually drives the price

- **Size and letter count.** Material and labor scale with every letter. "JOE'S" costs less than "JOE'S FAMILY RESTAURANT" at the same letter height, and a 36-inch set costs more than an 18-inch set of the same name.
- **Lighting style.** Front-lit is the workhorse. Halo-lit letters need deeper fabrication and standoff mounting, which adds cost. Combination front-and-halo sits at the top.
- **Mounting method.** Flush-mounted letters attach straight to the fascia. A raceway adds the cost of the box but simplifies wiring and wall penetrations, and many Houston landlords require one in their sign criteria.
- **Building height and access.** A sign that needs a larger lift or after-hours install costs more to hang than one a standard bucket truck reaches from the parking lot.
- **Permits and engineering.** Exterior signs need a sign permit, and the city also charges separately for plan review and inspections. Large or unusual installs can require stamped engineering.

## The Houston-specific costs most guides skip

National price guides stop at fabrication. In Greater Houston, two local factors move the final number:

- **Where the building sits.** The City of Houston, Katy, Sugar Land, Pearland, The Woodlands, and unincorporated Harris County each run their own sign permitting, with different fees, review times, and rules. Our [sign permit guide](/sign-permits) breaks down every jurisdiction we work in. We handle the permit either way, but the jurisdiction affects both cost and timeline.
- **Gulf Coast wind load.** Signs here are engineered for coastal wind requirements. On most storefront sets this is baked into the fabrication, but large letters on tall fascias can trigger stamped engineering that inland shops never deal with.

## How to keep the cost down

- **Lead with a logo mark, not a long name.** A short name plus a distinctive logo element often reads better and costs less than spelling everything out.
- **Choose front-lit unless halo earns its keep.** Halo looks premium at night on the right wall. If your fascia is busy or your hours end at 6, front-lit does the job for less.
- **Ask your landlord for the sign criteria early.** Finding out about a required raceway after design means redoing work. We read your lease criteria before we design.
- **Start the permit early.** Most City of Houston sign permits take about 4 to 11 days. Building that into the schedule costs nothing; discovering it at the end delays your opening.
- **Reface instead of rebuild when rebranding.** If your existing letter shells are sound, new faces and vinyl can rebrand the sign for far less than a new set.

## Why the cheapest quote is usually missing something

## Common questions

**Do these prices include installation and permits?**
Yes. Every range on this page reflects the installed project, with permitting and installation included. That's also how we quote.

**Why do halo-lit letters cost more than front-lit?**
Each letter is fabricated as a deeper unit, mounted on standoffs, and tuned so the wall glow reads evenly. More fabrication and install time, higher price, premium result.

**Is a raceway cheaper than flush mounting?**
Not usually cheaper, but often required. The raceway itself adds cost, while saving wall penetrations and wiring time. If your landlord's criteria require one, that decision is already made.

**How long does a channel letter project take?**
Most projects run a few weeks from approved design to install, with the City of Houston permit typically taking 4 to 11 days of that. Tell us your opening date up front and we'll tell you honestly if we can hit it.
